# Vernier caliper — volume and density of a rectangular block

> This README is AI-generated.

An interactive, single-page lesson on the vernier caliper practical: measuring the length, breadth and height of a rectangular block, then finding its volume and density.

Live site: https://sonicunligmated.github.io/vernier-caliper-lesson/

## Sections

- **Aim and outcomes** — the aim, intended learning outcomes and where caliper measurements matter in medicine.
- **Theory** — the parts of the caliper, main and vernier scale divisions, least count (1 MSD − 1 VSD = 0.01 cm), MSR, VSR, total = MSR + (VSR × L.C), zero error and its correction. Includes an interactive reading trainer.
- **Method** — apparatus and the procedure step by step with figures.
- **Formula and results** — an example observation table and the calculation of volume and density (cm → cm³ → g/cm³).
- **MCQs** — twenty questions with shuffled choices and feedback.
- **Practical** — a simulated vernier caliper and digital balance:
  - sliding jaw moved by drag, thumb wheel, scroll wheel, arrow keys or fine-step buttons, with the vernier drawn to scale so the coinciding mark is exact;
  - outside jaws, inside jaws, depth rod, locking screw and thumb wheel;
  - magnified view of the main and vernier scales;
  - optional zero check with the jaws closed;
  - numbered tray of four blocks (each returns only to its own spot); a block cannot go between jaws that are too narrow and falls out of jaws that are far too wide;
  - choice of length, breadth or height, with the block turning to present that dimension; the jaw stops at the block's face and warns when pressed too hard;
  - typed MSR and VSR readings validated with hints;
  - weighing on the balance with tare, then a checked calculation of V and ρ with hints for common slips;
  - a guided tutorial with Skip step and Stop, and Start over.

## Example values

The worked example uses an aluminium block: l = 6.2 + 4 × 0.01 = 6.24 cm, b = 3.5 + 2 × 0.01 = 3.52 cm, h = 2.1 + 8 × 0.01 = 2.18 cm, V = 6.24 × 3.52 × 2.18 ≈ 47.88 cm³, m = 129.30 g, ρ ≈ 2.70 g/cm³.

## Model notes

- 1 MSD = 0.1 cm; 10 vernier divisions span 9 MSD (1 VSD = 0.09 cm); least count 0.01 cm; range 0–15 cm.
- Practical blocks: aluminium, oak, brass and acrylic, with sizes to 0.01 cm and masses consistent with typical densities.
- The simulated caliper has zero error nil.
