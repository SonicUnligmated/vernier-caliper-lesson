Physics practical · Vernier caliper

Volume and density of a rectangular block

Aim

To measure the length, breadth and height of the given rectangular object, and hence find its volume and density.

Learning outcomes

By the end of this session, students should be able to:

  • Use a vernier caliper to measure the dimensions of solid objects and determine their volume and density.
  • Represent data with accuracy.

Why it matters in medicine

Tools, implants and prosthetics

Surgical instruments, bone screws, plates and prosthetic parts are checked against their stated sizes with calipers.

Bone and tissue thickness

In research, the thickness of bones and tissue samples is measured to a tenth of a millimetre.

Orthopaedics

Orthopaedic teams use calipers for precise measurements of bones, joints and the implants that replace them.

Theory

A vernier caliper has two scales. The main scale on the beam is marked in millimetres. A short vernier scale on the sliding jaw splits one millimetre into ten equal steps, so lengths can be read to 0.1 mm, which is 0.01 cm.

The parts

Outside jaws

The large lower jaws grip an object from outside: lengths, breadths, heights and diameters.

Inside jaws

The small upper jaws open outwards inside a hole or a slot to measure its width.

Depth rod

A thin rod that slides out of the end of the beam to measure the depth of a hole or a step.

Lock and wheel

The thumb wheel moves the sliding jaw finely; the locking screw holds it still for reading.

Least count

1 MSD= 1 mm = 0.1 cm
10 VSD= 9 MSD, so 1 VSD = 0.9 mm = 0.09 cm
L.C = 1 MSD − 1 VSD = 0.1 − 0.09 = 0.01 cm
or L.C = 1 MSDnumber of vernier divisions = 0.1 cm10 = 0.01 cm

Each vernier division is 0.01 cm shorter than a main scale division. That small difference is what the vernier measures.

Reading the two scales

MSR · main scale reading

The main scale mark just before the vernier zero, in cm. If the zero sits between 6.2 and 6.3, the MSR is 6.2 cm.

VSR · vernier scale reading

The number of the vernier mark that lines up best with any main scale mark, counted in divisions (0 to 10). Multiply by L.C to change it to cm.

Total reading = MSR + (VSR × L.C)

Moving the jaw by 0.01 cm shifts the coinciding vernier mark along by one division.

Zero error

With the jaws closed and nothing between them, the vernier zero should line up with the main scale zero. If it does, the zero error is nil. If not, the closed reading is the zero error, and it is removed from every reading.

corrected reading = observed reading − zero error

For example, with a zero error of + 0.03 cm, an observed 4.56 cm becomes 4.53 cm; with − 0.04 cm it becomes 4.60 cm.

Gentle contact

The jaws should just touch the faces of the block. Squeezing hard tilts the sliding jaw and can dent soft materials, so the reading is no longer the true size.

Read square-on

Look straight down at the scales. Viewing at an angle (parallax) makes a different pair of marks seem to line up.

From dimensions to density

V = l × b × h

Volume of a rectangular block, in cm³, from the length, breadth and height in cm.

ρ = mV

Density: mass in g over volume in cm³, giving g/cm³.

Method

Apparatus

Steps

Good practice
  • Square to the jaws. Hold the block flat against the fixed jaw, so the measured edge is parallel to the beam.
  • Light contact. Close with the thumb wheel until the jaw just touches; do not squeeze.
  • Lock before reading. The locking screw stops the jaw moving while the scales are read.
  • Read square-on. Look straight at the vernier to avoid parallax.
  • MSR in cm, VSR in divisions. Only VSR is multiplied by the least count.

Formula & results

Calculation

Common slips

Multiplying the MSRs only

6.2 × 3.5 × 2.1 = 45.57 cm³ leaves out every vernier part. Use the total readings.

Adding instead of multiplying

6.24 + 3.52 + 2.18 = 11.94 is a length, not a volume. V = l × b × h.

VSR written as cm

A VSR of 4 means 4 divisions = 0.04 cm. Adding 4 cm to the MSR would be far off.

Density upside down

V ÷ m = 0.370 is volume per gram. Density is mass per volume: m ÷ V.

Wrong main scale mark

The MSR is the mark just before the vernier zero, not the nearest one or the one after it.

Squeezing the jaws

Pressing hard tilts the jaw and changes the reading. Close gently, lock, then read.
